## 1. Why the format works

Four mechanics, in order of importance:

1. **A constant visual anchor.** The same foot lands in the same part of the
   frame on every cut. The anchor stays put while the world changes behind it.
   Break this and the reel collapses into a normal montage.
2. **Beat lock.** Every transition lands on a percussive hit. No exceptions —
   one cut that drifts off-beat is felt even by viewers who cannot name why.
3. **Escalation.** Locations get progressively more striking and the cuts get
   progressively faster.
4. **A held payoff.** The final step stops moving and opens wide, right as the
   track drops. Motion has to *stop* for the payoff to read.

## 2. The Maldives adaptation

Do not restage Japan. The Maldives has one thing Japan does not: a continuous
gradient from **dry land → wet sand → shallow water → open ocean → underwater**.
Build the escalation on that gradient, so the format is motivated by the
location rather than borrowed from it.

**Anchor:** a bare right foot, stepping left-to-right, landing at roughly 55%
frame width / 70% frame height.

**Three-act shape:**

| Act | Steps | Surface | Pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Land | 1–5 | jetty planks, dry sand, palm shade | ~0.8 s/cut |
| II — Edge | 6–11 | wet sand, tide sheet, ankle water, glass floor | ~0.6 s/cut |
| III — Water | 12–15 | sandbank, sunset reflection, step off the deck | ~0.4 s/cut |
| Payoff | 16 | underwater reveal, motion stops, wide | hold 2.5 s |

The mid-reel twist: around step 9 the foot lands **on water** — freeze one frame
at contact, let the splash bloom, then continue. That single beat is what people
will comment on.

## 3. Shot list

Sixteen steps, three takes each. Target runtime 22–26 s.

1. Seaplane float / dock plank — arrival
2. Wooden jetty, slats running to the horizon
3. Dry white sand, footprint left behind
4. Palm shadow across sand
5. Resort path, greenery either side
6. Wet sand, tide sheet mirroring the sky
7. Ankle-deep turquoise, clear bottom
8. Villa steps descending into the lagoon
9. **Freeze/splash beat** — foot meets open water
10. Overwater villa glass floor, fish below
11. Sandbank strip, water on both sides
12. Swing or hammock in shallow water
13. Golden-hour wet sand, full reflection
14. Sunset silhouette, sun behind the ankle
15. Deck edge, foot leaving the frame
16. **Payoff** — underwater, wide, ocean opening out

### Filming rules

- **4K 60 fps** (1080p60 minimum). 60 fps gives clean speed ramps and crop room
  for 9:16.
- **Lock exposure and white balance per location.** Auto-exposure pumping
  mid-step ruins the cut.
- Each clip 3–5 s: foot enters, lands centre, continues out.
- **Same landing position every time.** Mark it on the screen with tape if
  needed. This is the entire trick.
- Same direction of travel, same foot, same anklet/jewellery — continuity.
- Same phone height and distance throughout. Brace on a knee or use a gimbal.
- Golden hour for most of it; reserve 11:00–14:00 only for the turquoise and
  underwater shots, when the water reads its most saturated.

## 4. Edit recipe

**VN — structure and timing**

1. New project, 60 fps, 9:16.
2. Drop the audio in first. Tap-mark every beat before touching the video.
3. Snap each clip's out-point to the **impact frame** — the frame the foot
   contacts the surface — and align it to a beat marker.
4. Per cut, one of: a 2–4 frame zoom punch, a whip-blur on the foot lift, or a
   mask wipe where the leg crosses the frame. Rotate between them; do not use
   the same one sixteen times.
5. Speed ramp across contact on the Act III cuts: 0.5× → 2× over ~0.15 s.
6. Grade last, one preset across every clip so it reads as a single film.

**InShot — finish**

7. Hook text on frame 1.
8. Final caption card over the payoff.
9. Export 1080×1920, highest bitrate available.

**Grade targets.** The two failure modes in the Maldives are blown highlights
and an overall cyan cast. Keep sand neutral-white, push teal only in the water
via HSL, lift shadows slightly, add mild grain.

## 5. Audio

- Pull from **Reels' own audio library** while it is trending — imported tracks
  do not get the same algorithmic pickup.
- Pick something with a clear hit on beats 1 and 3.
- Trim so the drop lands exactly on step 16.
- 22–26 s total. Longer bleeds retention on this format.

## 6. Hook, caption, loop

- **Hook text on frame 1**, e.g. "the maldives in 16 steps". Do not open on
  step 1 — open on the most arresting step you shot.
- **Loop it.** Match the last frame to the first so it runs seamlessly. Looping
  is the reach engine for this format; a reel that loops cleanly gets counted
  as multiple views.
- **Caption tone:** conversational and low-effort, matching the reference's
  register. e.g. "maldives said take one more step 🩵"
- **Hashtags:** 3 large + 5 mid + 3 niche. Not 30.

## 7. Schedule

| Phase | Time |
|---|---|
| Pre-production, location scout | 1 day |
| Shoot (two golden hours + one midday block) | 2 days |
| Edit | 4–6 h |

## 8. Risks

- **Salt, sand, water.** Waterproof case; rinse the case in fresh water after
  every session.
- **Underwater housing required.** Phone "water resistance" ratings do not
  survive depth or salt.
- **Drone permits.** Many Maldives resort areas restrict drone use — clear it
  with the resort first if any aerial is planned.
- **Do not clone the reference.** Same mechanic is fine and expected; the same
  audio *and* caption *and* shot order reads as a rip and gets called out in
  comments.
- **Shoot everything yourself.** Downloaded filler clips are a copyright
  exposure and visibly break the anchor continuity.
